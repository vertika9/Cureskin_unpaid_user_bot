**Summary:** This week's audit covered 3,645 total bot exchanges, of which 116 (3.2%) were non-answers routed to doctor-call flows and 88 timed out. Of the 3,441 answered exchanges, a random sample of 1000 was evaluated: the bot was rated unsatisfactory on 36.5% of responses (95% CI ±3.0%). The most frequent failure modes were vague or generic responses that did not address the user's specific concern, misunderstood questions (particularly in Hindi/Hinglish), and cases where the bot failed to acknowledge affordability concerns or specific product questions. Notable failure patterns:
- "User says she can't pay online/has no account and wants to pay after arrival; bot's answer is muddled, mentioning a non-refundable ₹50 online payment and not clearly addressing the situation."
- "User said the order is for face, not hair, but the bot ignored the clarification and listed generic products without addressing what the user meant."
- "The bot claims the first follow-up is 14 days after starting and a "detailed report" is given, which is unverified or specific, and it does not clearly answer when the doctor will follow up."
- "User wants to return the order because there's no result; the bot gives a vague, confusing answer and doesn't clearly state the return policy (it says "no money-back guarantee" elsewhere)."
- "The bot contradicts itself, saying treatment is available and then that it is out of scope, and doesn't clearly say whether the marks and moles will go away."
