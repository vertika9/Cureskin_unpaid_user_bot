**Summary:** This week's audit covered 3,645 total bot exchanges, of which 116 (3.2%) were non-answers routed to doctor-call flows and 88 timed out. Of the 3,441 answered exchanges, a random sample of 1000 was evaluated: the bot was rated unsatisfactory on 11.7% of responses (95% CI ±2.0%). The most frequent failure modes were vague or generic responses that did not address the user's specific concern, misunderstood questions (particularly in Hindi/Hinglish), and cases where the bot failed to acknowledge affordability concerns or specific product questions. Notable failure patterns:
- "User said "Ok mam" (acknowledgement), but bot launched into unprompted product recommendations instead of offering further help or closing appropriately."
- "User said "मुझे नहीं समझ में आ रहा है" (I don't understand), which is vague. Bot assumed they meant ordering process and explained that, but the user's confusion wasn't clarified—could have asked a clarifying question first."
- "Products listed (Derma Rescue Cream, Barrier Restructure Cream, Hair Revive Serum, Hair Revive Capsules) are generic/made-up names not verifiable as actual Cure products; misleading product information."
- "Claims that "Radiance Body Soap" lightens skin and exfoliates are misleading health claims without substantiation; "fairness soap" claims are problematic in a dermatology context."
- "Statement about COD requiring a ₹50 non-refundable confirmation charge is vague and potentially misleading; this detail about payment terms should be verified against actual Cure policy before being stated as fact."
